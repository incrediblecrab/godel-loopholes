# A consent-aware, closed-world Article V search

**This is a replication extension, not a discovered constitutional loophole.**
The finite search makes the interpretation table in `ratification-price.md`
executable, replaces abstract ratification support with identified states, and
requires legal changes to follow explicit proposal and ratification events.
It does not identify what Gödel privately had in mind.

Research date: September 8, 2026. Constitutional vantage: December 5, 1947.
Later scholarship can inform the analysis of that snapshot; later constitutional
amendments and judicial holdings are not silently inserted into its rules.

## What was actually run

The canonical numerical record is `search/article_v_results.json`, generated by
`search/article_v_search.py`. It records the source hashes, environment,
interpretations, support sets, saturated graph sizes, shortest witnesses, and
independent bounded solver verdicts. Re-running with `--check` compares the
entire result except environment-version metadata; it does not rewrite it.

The grid has sixteen interpretations, three support profiles, and three
terminal predicates. That is **48 configurations and 144 target queries**:
**54 reachable and 90 unreachable within the declared finite graphs**. All
144 agree with a separately implemented SMT encoding at a bound of four
proposal/ratification/withdrawal operations. Two additional ablations contribute
six separately reported queries, not six additional interpretations.

Those are counts of model questions, **not** independently discovered legal
paths, tested real-world coalitions, or constitutional impossibility results.
Changing only the screening stage often repeats the same constitutional
outcome. No percentage of the Constitution has been searched.

Two existing Isabelle experiments were also rerun, using the repository's
unchanged generator and isolated scratch storage: `minimal` and `noamd1`.
The six-axiom sufficiency result reproduces with a consistency countermodel,
and all three published target theorems plus the five intermediate amendment
lemmas survive removal of the original step-one axioms in a consistent theory.
These reproduce the existing instrument finding; they are not new discoveries.

## Model and correspondence

The primary text is [Article V](https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/article-5/),
read on September 8, 2026. Its state-ratification requirement and its
**"without its Consent"** exception are distinct conditions. The existing
`threshold-arithmetic.md` supplies the pre-1947 congressional interpretation.
The resulting inputs are the 1947 statutory chamber sizes and the
three-fourths requirement for the forty-eight states.

| Representation | Meaning and limitation |
|---|---|
| `proviso` | Whether the equal-suffrage restriction is in force, not whether it happens to be obeyed. |
| `equal_senate` | Equality of state Senate suffrage. The sole unequal-suffrage action affects the designated state numbered 47; this is not an enumeration of all apportionment plans. |
| `legislative_independence`, `judicial_independence` | Whether those powers remain outside the executive. Their simultaneous removal is the Z&B-style concentration-of-powers target, not a complete political-science definition of dictatorship. |
| `executive_elections` | Continued electoral replacement of the executive. Removing this alone does not imply concentration of all three powers. It is a distinct target-sensitivity experiment. |
| `ratifiers_required` | The currently operative number of ratifying states. The initial requirement can change only under the old rule. |
| `ratifiers`, `consenters` | Separate sets of identified states. Neither a count of individual legislators nor an assumption about the electorate's support. |
| `pending` | At most one proposed amendment. No parallel proposals, rescissions, disputed deadlines, certification dispute, or constitutional convention procedure is modeled. |

`Votes` enforces quorum and two-thirds voting conditions. Ratification may be
labeled legislative or convention-based, but the internal law of neither state
legislatures nor state conventions is encoded. Both have the same Article V
state count; their political and individual-person costs need not be equal.
Congressional proposal is the only proposal route implemented.

The grid's ratifying and consenting sets are the same prefix of state
identifiers, at three sizes: just below the initial threshold, exactly at it,
and all states. This is a declared scenario choice, not a derived identity
between ratification and consent. Separate controls change the two sets
independently and hold their cardinalities equal while changing the affected
state's identity.

The action alphabet is explicit in the artifact: repeal/restore the proviso,
concentrate/restore separated powers, remove/restore executive elections,
change/restore equal Senate suffrage, lower/restore the ratification threshold,
and two combined instruments used to test self-authorization. These abstract
effects are stipulations about hypothetical amendments, **not drafted amendments
whose legal sufficiency has been verified**.

## Interpretations that change the answer

Four independent switches produce the sixteen grid interpretations.

| Switch | Alternatives |
|---|---|
| Equal-suffrage scope | Formal equality of state votes, or functional protection of the Senate's legislative role as well. |
| Protection of the proviso | Ordinary repeal is possible, or repealing the proviso itself requires every state's consent. The latter is a modeled self-entrenchment reading, not a claim of absolute unamendability even with unanimous consent. |
| An implicit democracy floor | Absent, or a substantive limit blocks removal of separated powers and executive electoral replacement. This is an intentionally stronger rival interpretation, not asserted to be settled 1947 law. |
| Screening stage | Substantive validity is tested at proposal, or at ratification. Proposal alone never changes the constitution in either variant. |

Ordinary self-amendment of the ratification rule is allowed in the main grid
and disabled in a separately labeled ablation. That permission is an assumption,
not a result of the search. The same is true of the chosen effect of each
hypothetical amendment. The model cannot decide which legal interpretation is
correct by running more paths through it.

Holding the democracy floor absent and using the threshold-sized support set,
the concentration-of-powers target reproduces the existing two-by-two:

| Suffrage scope | Ordinary proviso repeal | Self-entrenched proviso |
|---|---|---|
| Formal | One ratification; repeal is unnecessary. | One ratification; the untouched proviso does not block this target. |
| Functional | Two successive ratifications; the separate repeal is necessary in this action alphabet. | Unreachable with this support set. |

This is not a vote among interpretations. Counting the favorable cells would
not produce a probability of legal validity.

With consent from every state and no implicit democracy floor, the direct
concentration amendment is a positive control: the proviso's consent exception
matters. Requiring ordinary ratification and requiring consent from every
affected state are not interchangeable.

The target-sensitivity result is equally important. Protecting equal suffrage
and even protecting the proviso itself does not, in this abstraction, protect
executive electoral replacement. The broad statement "step one is necessary
for dictatorship" therefore exceeds what a proof about one concentration
predicate can establish.

## What the explicit transition system adds

**No spontaneous change.** Proposal and withdrawal preserve every
constitutional field. Ratification changes only the effect fields of its
matching pending amendment. The rest persist by construction. Deleting the
repeal actions therefore blocks the functional-scope concentration path at
threshold support; it does not leave the proviso free to disappear.

That is a different question from the older repaired model's open-world
independence result. Failure of entailment, existence of a satisfying endpoint,
and reachability by authorized operations are three different properties.

**No same-instrument bootstrap.** A combined repeal-and-concentration instrument
is assessed against the operative, pre-amendment proviso. Likewise, an
instrument lowering the ratification threshold cannot count votes against the
lower threshold before it is validly enacted. The below-threshold profile
reaches no changed constitutional state.

**Consent follows identity.** A suffrage change affecting one state is blocked
when that state has not consented, even if enough other states have consented.
Exchanging one consenter for the affected state changes the answer without
changing the number of consenters. This is a stronger control than merely
checking a unanimity count for the all-powers target.

**Repeated rounds are not distinct people.** A two-amendment witness may reuse
the same ratifying states. Two rounds do not imply twice as many distinct
states, legislators, or voters. The search does not estimate those people's
preferences or their internal ratification thresholds.

## Exhaustiveness and independent checking

Breadth-first search continues until its queue is empty, using the complete
immutable constitutional state and pending amendment as the visited key.
Consequently a reported unreachable target is excluded at **every path length
in that finite transition graph**, not merely at the first few steps.
Cycles introduced by restoration and withdrawal are included.

The SMT encoder independently implements the guards and persistence equations.
It shares the amendment-effect catalogue, the declared inputs, and the terminal
definitions as specification data. Thus agreement checks two implementations
of the same interpretation; it does not independently validate that
interpretation against the law.

The solver cross-check is bounded. Its `UNSAT` means no path within the stated
operation bound; the unbounded-within-the-finite-graph statement comes from
BFS saturation, not from SMT. A unit test deliberately asks for the two-step
result at a too-short bound and obtains `UNSAT` although the graph has a longer
witness. `UNKNOWN` remains a separate outcome and makes the result checker exit
inconclusively rather than report impossibility.

Every SMT witness is replayed through the concrete transition implementation.
The committed artifact keeps deterministic BFS witnesses rather than arbitrary
solver assignments. Initial satisfiability and direct-consent positive controls
guard against vacuous exclusion.

## Reproduction

From the repository root, using the existing environment:

```sh
.venv/bin/python -m unittest discover -s analysis/united-states-1947/search -p 'test_article_v.py' -v
.venv/bin/python analysis/united-states-1947/search/article_v_search.py --check
(cd analysis/united-states-1947/search && ../../../.venv/bin/python -m article_v_mutations)
```

Use `--write` instead of `--check` to regenerate the result artifact after a
deliberate model change. Inspect that change rather than treating regeneration
as validation. The new work does not modify the published-model transcription
or its existing ablation generator.

The mutation command plants four defects in disposable copies: bypassing the
ratification threshold, replacing state identity with consent cardinality,
allowing an instrument to use its own repeal, and making a proposal change law
before ratification. Each unmodified control must exit zero; each mutant must
produce a behavioral assertion failure and exit one. An import error does not
count as detecting a defect. The measured controls are recorded separately in
`search/article_v_mutation_results.json`.

## What remains outside the evidence

There is no kernel-certified proof of this Python model's correspondence with
constitutional law. There is no historical backtest in the interpretation grid,
no model of coercion or political persuasion, and no search of the whole
statute book, standing rules, executive succession, or judicial practice.
Constitutional changes outside the chosen alphabet can produce different
answers. State admission and alteration of the state universe are excluded.

The principal unresolved issue is still legal semantics: which restrictions
apply to the power to amend the restrictions themselves? The model localizes
that disagreement; it does not settle it. None of its witnesses establishes a
novel loophole, a small-coalition route around ratification, or historical
attribution to Gödel.
