# The KWEG bridge

## Texts and recensions

The B-VG is cited from `corpus/austria-1920/10-01-1920-stgbl-450-b-vg-oeaw-machine-transcription.txt`, StGBl. 450/1920, a machine transcription. No claim here depends on its punctuation.

The KWEG is cited from `corpus/austria-1920/07-24-1917-rgbl-307-kriegswirtschaftliches-ermaechtigungsgesetz-wikisource-transcription.txt`, RGBl. 307/1917, a proofread transcription.

Steps 2, 4, 5, 6 and 7 below rest on `academia/interwar-collapse.md` rather than on a corpus text, because the corpus does not hold the documents they turn on. Each is marked where it occurs. This chain is longer than Germany's and less of it is certified, and both facts are properties of the case rather than of the effort spent on it.

## The path

**Step 1. The constitution defers its own transition, and subordinates its own commencement to the deferral.**

Article 150 provides that the transition to the federal constitution introduced by the law is regulated by a separate constitutional law entering into force at the same time. Article 151(1) provides that the law enters into force on the day of the National Council's first sitting, insofar as no exceptions are established by the law designated in Article 150.

The second sentence is the one that matters. A constitution that defers transitional detail to a companion statute has done something ordinary. A constitution whose own entry into force is expressly subject to exceptions defined in that companion statute has put a document it does not contain ahead of itself in the order of authority, for as long as the transitional provisions live. In Austria's case that turned out to be permanently.

Legal status: settled. Nothing here is contested; it is what the articles say.

**Step 2. The transitional law preserves pre-1920 law in force.**

The bridge is § 7(2) of the Übergangsgesetz, StGBl. 451/1920, which carried forward the existing body of law rather than requiring re-enactment. This is `academia/interwar-collapse.md`, not the corpus. The document is absent from the snapshot for the reason given in this folder's README, and the missing certification is at exactly the link that does the work.

Legal status: settled as a matter of history. Not verified against a 1920 recension by this project.

**Step 3. A 1917 imperial enabling act therefore survives into a republic, and its check points at a body that no longer exists.**

The KWEG § 1 empowers the government, by ordinance, to take the measures necessary to promote and restore economic life, to avert economic damage and to supply the population with food and other necessities, for the duration of the extraordinary circumstances brought about by the war.

Three limits, all real, and each fails differently.

The subject matter is economic and named, which is a genuine restriction, and `academia/interwar-collapse.md` records Tálos documenting which ordinances exceeded it. That limit was breached rather than evaded, which is worth separating from the rest: it is the one place in this chain where someone simply broke the rule.

The duration is the interesting one, because it is not the duration of the war. It is the duration of the extraordinary circumstances the war brought about, and circumstances have no armistice. Nothing in the statute names who determines that they have ended. A power limited by a condition with no defined terminus and no named judge of the terminus is limited in form only, and the arrival of a global depression made the claim that extraordinary economic circumstances persisted easier to sustain in 1934 than it had been in 1925.

The check in § 3 is unconditional in appearance and conditional in fact. Ordinances must be laid before the Reichsrat at the end of each calendar quarter if it is assembled, and otherwise at its coming together. The obligation's deadline is defined by the body's own sitting, so a body that does not sit generates no deadline. Section 5 then requires that ordinances not laid before the Reichsrat by the § 3 deadline be set aside, which makes the sanction conditional on a deadline that is conditional on assembly. The enforcement provision inherits the defect it exists to cure.

There is a further problem in § 3 that no drafter could have been expected to prevent and that a reader in 1920 could not have missed. The body named is the Reichsrat, the imperial parliament, which ceased to exist with the empire in 1918. After that date the only check in the statute names an institution that is gone. Whether and how the reference was transposed to a republican body is a question for the transitional law, which is the document this project does not hold.

Legal status: the text is settled. The consequence of the 1918 discontinuity for § 3 is contested and this project has not resolved it.

**Step 4. The constitution acquires a proper emergency power, correctly checked, and it is never used.**

The 1929 Novelle introduced a presidential emergency ordinance power in Article 18, and `academia/interwar-collapse.md` records Wiederin establishing that it required the approval of a standing parliamentary subcommittee and that it has never been used to this day. It was ignored in favour of the 1917 statute.

This step is in the path because of what it rules out, not what it enables. The well-designed instrument sat unused beside the badly-aged one, and the attacker took the one with the weaker check. Hardening Article 18 further would have changed nothing.

Legal status: settled, and independently confirmed at one point by the corpus, since Article 18 of the 1920 text has two paragraphs and no emergency power at all.

**Step 5. The chamber is made unable to reconvene through a gap in its own rules of procedure.**

On March 4, 1933 all three presidents of the Nationalrat resigned in succession during a contested vote, and the rules of procedure had no provision for the resulting situation, leaving no one with authority to reconvene. This is `academia/interwar-collapse.md`.

The operator of every parliamentary check in the system was removed at this step, and it was removed by a defect in a document that is not a constitution and is not a statute. What followed, the declaration of self-dissolution and the prevention of reconvening by force, was made available by the gap and was not authorised by it.

Legal status: the gap is settled. The characterisation of what was done with it is contested, which is why hdgö's phrase for it is a juridical coup rather than either a lawful act or a plain one.

**Step 6. The Constitutional Court is disabled by an attack on its quorum.**

`academia/interwar-collapse.md` records the sequence: parliament-nominated members resigned in May 1933, the government decreed that parliament-nominated members could sit only if all of them were present, which the resignations had made impossible, and further resignations took the court below quorum.

The court that could have ruled on Steps 3 and 5 was removed by a rule about who must be in the room. No provision of the constitution was amended to achieve it.

Legal status: `academia/interwar-collapse.md` records hdgö calling this unconstitutional outright, which makes it the second step in this chain that is not merely contested.

**Step 7. A constitution is promulgated by ordinance under the 1917 statute.**

The 1934 constitution was brought into force by ordinance under the KWEG. The statute's economic subject-matter limit, its duration limit and its parliamentary check were all still on the books, unrepealed, at the moment a wartime economic enabling act was used to promulgate a constitution.

Legal status: contested, and the contest is one-sided. `academia/interwar-collapse.md` records Balthasar making the minority case that it was defensible, and notes most scholars disagree.

## Minimum coalition

Germany's path produced a number: 444 deputies voted for the Enabling Act, a figure `academia/interwar-collapse.md` records from the Bundestag's own account, and the article's composed fractions could in principle have been satisfied by about forty-four percent of the chamber.

Austria's path produces no parliamentary number at all. The 1934 constitution was promulgated by government ordinance, so the minimum coalition is the cabinet that could agree to issue it, plus whatever was needed to keep the Nationalrat from reassembling and the Constitutional Court below quorum. There is no fraction of any legislature in the answer.

That is the comparison the two cases exist to produce. A method that measures constitutional robustness by the size of the majority needed to change the constitution rates Austria's 1920 document well and is not wrong about the document. The document was not the route.

## Falsifier

The claim is that the authorising norm for the 1934 constitution sat outside the 1920 constitution, was reached through it via the transitional law, and that the checks which should have stopped the chain failed for reasons visible in texts that a constitution-only corpus does not contain.

It would be wrong if § 7(2) of StGBl. 451/1920 does not in fact preserve the KWEG, which would break the chain at its uncertified link and is the first thing a competent opponent should attack. It would be substantially weakened if the transitional law transposed § 3's reference from the Reichsrat to a republican body in terms that kept the check live, since the argument about the defunct referent would fall while the argument about conditional deadlines would survive. It would be wrong in a more interesting way if the 1934 promulgation rested on some other authority than the KWEG, in which case the whole bridge is a coincidence of chronology.

Resolving the first two requires putting StGBl. 451/1920 in the corpus at a certified 1920 recension, which is the highest-value acquisition this project currently has open.

## The edit that closes it

There is no amendment to the 1920 constitution that closes this, and that is the finding.

The edits that would have closed it are three, and none of them is constitutional text. The transitional law needed a repeal schedule naming which pre-1920 enabling statutes lapsed, rather than a general preservation of existing law, because a general preservation carries forward instruments written for a state that no longer exists along with everything else. The Nationalrat's rules of procedure needed a provision for the simultaneous resignation of all presiding officers, which is the sort of case that looks absurd until someone resigns into it. The Constitutional Court's composition rules needed a quorum that could not be lowered by the departure of members, since a court whose members can disable it by leaving has delegated its own existence to them.

A project that reads constitutions and asks what sentence should have been added to the constitution would produce none of these. That is the specific way this case is a test of method rather than an illustration of it.
