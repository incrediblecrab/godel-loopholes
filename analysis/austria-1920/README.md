# Austria, 1920

This is the second validation case and it tests something the German case cannot. Germany's path ran through the constitution. Austria's did not. The instrument that carried the 1934 constitution into force was an imperial statute from 1917, passed under a monarchy that no longer existed, and a method that reads only the constitution would never see it.

## Vantage

The snapshot is Austria at the constitution's commencement in 1920, and it holds two files in `corpus/austria-1920/`: the Bundes-Verfassungsgesetz at StGBl. 450/1920, and the Kriegswirtschaftliches Ermächtigungsgesetz at RGBl. 307/1917.

The B-VG file is a machine transcription. Nothing in this folder makes a claim about its punctuation, and no candidate that depends on a comma may be raised from it until a proofread recension is in the corpus. That constraint is worth stating plainly in a project that reads punctuation, because the temptation is to treat a clean-looking text as a certified one. The file also preserves the source's own line breaks, which is why quotations here are joined rather than reproduced as laid out.

The KWEG file is a proofread transcription and carries one known defect, recorded in `corpus/README.md`, in the signature block rather than the operative text.

## The link this folder cannot document

The chain runs from the B-VG through the Übergangsgesetz to the KWEG. The middle document is not in the corpus, and its absence is deliberate.

The text available for it is a recension published at BGBl. 368/1925 and consolidated to 2012. Filing that under a 1920 snapshot would assert that its wording is the wording that existed in 1920, and that assertion cannot be certified. The substance of the relevant provision is very likely unchanged, since no amendment annotation for it exists between 1920 and 1925, but likely is not the standard, and punctuation certainly cannot be carried across a recension boundary.

So this analysis states a chain whose second link it cannot show. That is recorded here as a limit on the conclusion rather than buried, and the findings below are arranged so that the ones resting on the corpus alone are separable from the one that does not.

## What holds without the missing link

The reconstruction is in `path-kweg-bridge.md`. Three of its findings depend only on files in the snapshot.

The B-VG subordinates its own entry into force to a document outside itself. Article 151(1) provides that the law enters into force on the day of the National Council's first sitting, insofar as no exceptions are established by the law designated in Article 150. A constitution that makes its own commencement conditional on exceptions set elsewhere has placed something ahead of itself, and that is visible from Article 151 alone without knowing what the other law said.

The KWEG's temporal limit is indexed to a condition with no defined end and no named judge of when it has ended. Section 1 empowers the government for the duration of the extraordinary circumstances brought about by the war, which is not the same as the duration of the war. Circumstances outlast wars. The empire that passed the statute was gone within sixteen months of the armistice and the statute was still being used in 1934.

The KWEG's only check is textually conditional on the checking body sitting. Section 3 requires ordinances to be laid before the Reichsrat quarterly if it is assembled, and otherwise at its coming together. If it does not come together, the deadline does not mature. Section 5 then provides that ordinances not laid before the Reichsrat by the Section 3 deadline must be set aside, which makes the sanction conditional on a deadline that is itself conditional on assembly. The enforcement clause inherits the defect it was meant to cure.

## The finding that is in a text nobody put in the corpus

The step that ended parliamentary government in Austria looks at first like the one place a textual method can never reach, and it is not. On March 4, 1933, during a contested vote, all three presidents of the Nationalrat resigned in succession, and the rules of procedure contained no provision for that situation, so no one was left with authority to reconvene the chamber. `academia/interwar-collapse.md` records the Haus der Geschichte Österreich's account and its verdict that a juridical coup had been completed.

That is an unhandled case in a written procedural rule, exploited by resigning into it. It is exactly the kind of defect this project exists to find, it was findable in advance by anyone who read the Geschäftsordnung asking what happens if every presiding officer resigns at once, and it is invisible to us because our corpus holds constitutions and statutes and not the rules by which chambers govern their own sittings.

The lesson is therefore not that textual methods have a ceiling here. It is that the corpus boundary was drawn in the wrong place. A constitution's most important check is often a body, and a body's ability to act is governed by its own procedural rules, so those rules are part of the constitutional machinery whatever their formal rank. This is an argument for widening the corpus, and it is the first such argument the project has generated from evidence rather than from taste.

What genuinely has no text is what came after: Dollfuß declaring the chamber to have dissolved itself and then preventing it from reconvening, by force where necessary. The gap made that available. It did not compel it.

The same pattern appears again two months later against the Constitutional Court, and it is a quorum attack of the same shape. `academia/interwar-collapse.md` records that parliament-nominated members resigned in sequence in May 1933, the government then decreed that parliament-nominated members could sit only if all of them were present, which the resignations had made impossible, and further resignations pushed the court below quorum. A composition rule was used to disable the body it constituted. Twice in one spring, the operator of a check was removed by arithmetic about who was in the room.

## Null results

The B-VG was searched for an unamendable clause or a substantive limit on constitutional amendment of the kind Germany lacked, on the theory that its presence would distinguish the two cases. That is not where the difference lies. The Austrian path did not go through the amendment procedure at all, so the presence or absence of a limit on amendment is not on the path, and time spent hardening the amendment article would have protected nothing. This is the clearest available demonstration that the location of the authorizing norm has to be established before the checks around it are assessed.

The KWEG was checked for a defect in its own drafting, on the pattern of the German decree's substituted adverb, and none was found. Its three limits are stated competently: the subject matter is economic and named, the duration is stated, the parliamentary check is unconditional in form. The instrument that carried a dictatorship was not badly drafted. It was well drafted for 1917 and then survived into a state it was not written for, which is a different failure and one that no amount of care at drafting time would have caught.
