<section aria-labelledby="it-reproduces">

## It reproduces

Zahoransky and Benzmüller built a model of Gödel&#8217;s argument in Isabelle/HOL, a proof assistant whose kernel checks every step. It compiles clean against Isabelle 2025 and discharges thirty theorems, including the three that carry the argument: no dictatorship under the Constitution as it stood, none after Article&nbsp;V is amended, and dictatorship reached at the third step with no rule broken.

Compiling is not reproducing, and the distance between them is the whole discipline. An inconsistent axiom set proves every formula, so a theory can discharge the dictatorship theorem while carrying no information at all. The thesis itself ships a deliberately inconsistent smoke-test theory that proves `False` on purpose, and our harness confirms it still does.

So the result was tested three further ways. Nitpick was asked to satisfy the axioms, and found a model. It was asked to refute `False`, and found a countermodel, so the axioms do not entail everything. Then it was asked for countermodels to the duals of all three targets, and found all three. Countermodels are semantic rather than tactical: they show no proof can exist, which is stronger than observing that a proof attempt failed.

</section>

<MarginNote head="Replication">

All 110 identifiers in our transcription appear in the thesis. Of 67 labelled formulas in the source, 65 appear in ours; the two that do not are an integer time representation the thesis introduces only to reject, and a false positive on the English word <em>proposition</em>. No axiom was omitted.

One deviation is deliberate: the bold-face connectives were renamed to avoid colliding with HOL built-ins. That is notation only, and it is the most likely site of a silent divergence, so it is flagged rather than buried.

<section aria-labelledby="now-take-it-apart">

## Now take it apart

A proof that checks tells you the conclusion follows. It does not tell you what it follows <em>from</em>. The model has <Value id="model.axioms.total" bare /> axioms, and the natural question — which ones is the proof using? — is not one the paper asks.

Ablation answers it by deletion. Remove an axiom, ask the machine whether the theorem still holds, and ask a model finder whether the reduced theory is still consistent. That second question is not optional. An inconsistent theory obligingly proves every theorem you were hoping to see.

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="what-that-actually-means">

## What that actually means

Gödel&#8217;s route has two steps. First amend Article&nbsp;V to remove the clause protecting equal Senate suffrage. Then use the unentrenched procedure to install a dictator. Step one is the idea. It is the part people repeat.

Delete the four axioms that carry out step one, and every published theorem and every intermediate lemma still proves, in a theory that is still consistent. What is lost is exactly one proposition: <code>{cost.value}</code> — the ratification event itself — which stops being entailed and becomes <em>independent</em>. It does not become false. It becomes undetermined, which is what happens when the axioms that forced it are withdrawn.

So step one is not free, and it is not load-bearing either. It costs one proposition, and no published result uses it.

<p class="correction">This is a narrower claim than earlier drafts of this work made. One said the model <em>cannot represent</em> repeal at all. Another said deleting step one costs <em>nothing</em>. Both were false, adversarial review said so, and the machine agreed with the reviewer both times. The corrections are on the record in <a href={blobUrl(INERT)}>{INERT.split('/').pop()}</a> rather than quietly absorbed.</p>

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="so-we-built-the-one-they-did-not">

## So we built the one they did not

A defect in an encoding is not a fact about the world. If step one does no work here, the question is whether that is Gödel&#8217;s problem or the model&#8217;s, and there is exactly one way to find out: build a model without the defect and run the same ablation against it.

The defect is precise. The dictatorship amendment being <em>proposed</em> at step two is written into the published model as an axiom. But that proposition is the thing step one exists to bring about. Assume it, and step one has nothing left to do.

So derive it instead. In the repaired model an attempted amendment becomes a valid proposal when it is an amendment, the legislature supports it, and either the entrenchment clause is not in force or the amendment leaves equal Senate suffrage alone. The dictatorship amendment fails that last test, so it cannot be validly proposed while the clause stands. The repeal amendment passes it — abolishing the proviso does not itself change anyone&#8217;s representation in the Senate — so it goes through while the clause is still in force, and takes the clause with it. Six links, every one of them derived rather than stipulated, ending where Gödel said it would.

Then run the same ablation on those <Value id="netadd.axioms.total" bare /> axioms. Remove the five stipulations that carry step one and `Dictatorship_t3` stops being entailed — without becoming false. It goes <em>independent</em>: the model finder returns one world where the dictatorship arrives and another where it does not, and the axioms no longer choose between them. <strong>In a model that derives what the published one assumes, step one is necessary.</strong> The finding above reverses.

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="did-we-rig-it">

## Did we rig it

The obvious objection is that the rule was chosen to produce that answer. A proposal rule written so that step one lies on the only path would be the same defect as the axiom it replaces, pointed the other way. Our assurance that we did not do that is worth nothing.

So it is not an assurance. Drop the entrenchment check from the rule — leaving attempt, amendment status and legislative support sufficient on their own — and the prover derives a contradiction with Article&nbsp;V&#8217;s proviso outright. A sufficient condition for a valid proposal and a necessary one cannot disagree about the same amendment. So every rule consistent with the clause carries the check, and the check is what puts step one on the path. It was forced by the text rather than selected for the result. The proof is two lines long and all of its weight is in what it rules out.

One rival encoding survives the same test, and it does not rescue the published model either: put the check at ratification rather than at proposal, and step one is still necessary, one link further down the chain.

<p class="epistemic">Two of the four machine claims behind this repair are not theorems. That <code>Dictatorship_t3</code> holds in the repaired model is certified by Isabelle&#8217;s kernel, and so is the contradiction above. That the theorem goes independent without step one, and that either theory is consistent at all, rest on a bounded model finder instead. A countermodel it returns is a real negative; a search it loses is a timeout, not a result. Treating the second as the first is how this project has been wrong before.</p>

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="why-this-route-cannot-find-what-we-are-looking-for" class="caveat">

## Why this route cannot find what we are looking for

Neither model finds a hole in the Constitution, and neither could. The limitation is structural rather than a matter of effort: they contain constitutional text and nothing else.

That means they cannot see Austria, which left the constitution entirely and governed through an unrepealed 1917 wartime statute, and they cannot see Italy, whose Statuto was never amended while the state was dismantled around it. <strong>Two of the three collapses on <a href={`${base}/precedents/`}>the precedents page</a> are invisible to them in principle.</strong>

The second gap is larger, and only half of it turned out to be repairable. That the <em>effect</em> of amending Article&nbsp;V is never derived — merely implemented by omitting an axiom — is the defect the repair above removes. What survives the repair is that neither model represents ratification by the states at all: the quantity <a href={`${base}/text/`}>the text page</a> is entirely about. At <Value id="states.count" bare /> states the requirement is <Value id="threshold.states.ratify" bare />, and in both models the states simply agree. A model that omits the constraint cannot report that the constraint is what stops you.

Read precisely, both formalizations prove a conditional: <em>if</em> the amendments described are attempted and supported, the rest follows by lawful steps. The repair moves one link of that chain out of assumption and into derivation. It does not move the support.

</section>

This is not a criticism of the paper, which scopes itself to a case example and says so. It is a specification for what a successor has to do: the node set has to extend past constitutional text to the statutes standing beside it and the chamber rules operating inside it.

</MarginNote>

## Two things the tools got right and we got wrong

A verified result is only as good as the correspondence between the formula and the sentence in English attached to it, and that correspondence is the one link in the chain no prover checks.
