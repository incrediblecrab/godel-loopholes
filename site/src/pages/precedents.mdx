<section aria-labelledby="depths">

<h2 id="depths">The three depths</h2>

The cases are usually told as one story about the 1930s. They are three different stories, and the difference is the part that matters to a project that proposes to search a constitution for a defect.

Only Germany&#8217;s path went through the constitution. Austria&#8217;s ran through a law the constitution never mentions. Italy&#8217;s ran through ordinary statutes while the constitutional text sat unamended from beginning to end.

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="filter">

<h2 id="filter">This is a test we set for ourselves, not a result</h2>

{filter.value} That is not a historical observation. It is a standing objection to the method this project is built on, and it is kept in front of us on purpose.

{filter.statement} A technique that recovers Germany and misses the other two has not been validated; it has been fitted to the one case that happens to suit it.

Italy is held out deliberately as the case where the method is <em>predicted to fail</em>. A version of this project that reported a finding there would have shown that its procedures generate results from any input, which is the specific failure everything here is built to avoid.

</section>

<section aria-labelledby="arithmetic">

<h2 id="arithmetic">The German case has arithmetic in it that nobody quotes</h2>

Article&nbsp;76 is described everywhere as a two-thirds amendment rule. It was two rules. Two thirds of the statutory membership had to be present, and two thirds of those present had to assent, and those fractions compose.

<p class="pull">Two thirds of two thirds is {gThreshold.value}. About forty-four per cent.</p>

A clause whose entire purpose is to stop a majority from rewriting the constitution could be satisfied by a minority of the chamber. This needs no source beyond the clause and multiplication, and it falls straight out of the same threshold procedure that produces the American numbers on <a href={`${base}/text/`}>the text page</a>.

The actual vote was nowhere near that minimum, and the honest reading of that matters. The usual telling is that excluding the eighty-one Communist deputies is what carried the Enabling Act. The Bundestag&#8217;s own account says the opposite.

</section>
