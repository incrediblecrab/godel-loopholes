<section aria-labelledby="denominator">

<h2 id="denominator">The denominator nobody states</h2>

{unstated.value} Two thirds of the membership and two thirds of those voting are different numbers, and the text does not choose between them.

That silence is not universal among constitutions of comparable ambition. The Weimar constitution's Article&nbsp;76 states its base explicitly: two thirds of the statutory membership present, and two thirds of those present assenting. The same fraction appears in both documents and only one of them says what it divides.

</section>

One number in that machine is worth stopping on. The House's <Value id="house.size" bare /> is the figure every threshold above is computed from, and no statute states it. The Act of August&nbsp;8, 1911 fixed the House at <Value id="house.size.1911" bare />. Arizona and New Mexico each added one on admission the following year, and every apportionment act since — 1929 and 1941 alike — has carried the total forward as "the then existing number of Representatives," without ever naming it. The figure is correct. Its provenance is a chain rather than a citation, which is the same shape as the unstated base above, one layer further down.

<section aria-labelledby="recension">

<h2 id="recension">A comma is not a triviality</h2>

Ten amendments were collated across <Value id="recension.witnesses" bare words /> independent witnesses, expecting them to be identical. On wording they agree completely, and every one of the {SPELLED_COUNT} differences that turned up is a comma or a letter. Nobody planted these; the collation was run to check that our copies matched.

<section aria-labelledby="silence">

<h2 id="silence">Twelve places the document says nothing</h2>

A silence is not the same as a permission, and it is not the same as a prohibition. It is a field the constitution declines to reserve to itself, where ordinary law governs and no constitutional provision constrains what that law may say. Enumerating them is cheap, and the result is a map of who actually holds each pen.
