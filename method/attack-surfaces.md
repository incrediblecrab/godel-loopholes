# Attack surfaces

This file partitions the problem. Everything in `analysis/` is expected to say which class its candidate falls in, and a candidate that fits none of them is either a new class, which is a finding in itself, or confused.

## Where the authorizing norm sits

The primary partition is not what a step does but where the rule permitting it lives. Three classes, and they are exhaustive by construction: a norm is either in the constitution or not, and if it is not, the constitution either reaches it or does not.

**Class 1, inside.** The constitution itself authorizes the step. Germany is the clean case. Article 76 permitted constitutional amendment by ordinary legislation with a two-thirds quorum and no substantive limit of any kind, and the Enabling Act of March 1933 was passed under it, its preamble certifying that the requirements of constitution-amending legislation had been met. Article 3 of that act then provided that Articles 68 to 77 of the constitution did not apply to laws made by the government, which removed Article 76 itself from the path. An amendment rule with no substantive floor authorized a law that switched the amendment rule off.

**Class 2, outside but reached through.** A clause in the constitution carries in, preserves, or defers to law that sits outside it. Austria is the case, and it is the one most likely to be missed. Article 150 of the B-VG defers the entire transition to a separate constitutional law; Article 151 makes the B-VG's own entry into force conditional on the exceptions that law may set. Section 7(2) of that transitional law carries the powers of the wartime economic enabling act of July 24, 1917 forward to the federal government and to individual ministers. In 1934 a complete new constitution was promulgated under the 1917 act. The chain is three documents long, only the first link is in the constitution, and the instrument at the end of it is an ordinary economic statute the constitution never names.

**Class 3, outside and unreached.** The constitution does not govern the field at all, and ordinary legislation does the work. Italy is the case. The Statuto Albertino declared itself perpetual and irrevocable and its eighty-four articles were never amended. An 1848 snapshot and a 1926 snapshot are the same file. Everything happened in ordinary statutes: the electoral law of 1923 handing two thirds of the Chamber to whichever list cleared a quarter of the vote, the law of December 1925 making the head of government answerable to the King alone, the law of January 1926 transferring legislative power to the executive.

The classes are not equally visible. A method that reads only constitutional text sees class 1 completely, sees class 2 only if it follows references outward, and is blind to class 3 by construction. This is why the three collapses are the test set and why Italy is the discriminating case: any procedure that recovers Germany and misses Italy has not been validated, it has been flattered.

## How a check fails

Given that a safeguard exists, the second partition asks which part of it gave way. A check has four components — a trigger, an operator who fires it, a scope, and a duration — so with the case where no check exists at all there are five ways to fail, and they are exhaustive over the anatomy.

**No check.** Weimar Article 76 carried no substantive limit whatever. Nothing was placed beyond amendment, there was no unamendable clause, and the entrenchment was purely procedural.

**The trigger is self-judged.** The condition that opens the power is assessed by the person who benefits from opening it. Nothing in Weimar Article 48 gave anyone but the President the judgment that public security was seriously disturbed.

**The operator can be disabled.** This is the failure that matters most and the one a naive method scores as safe. Weimar Article 48(3) required that emergency measures be revoked on the demand of the Reichstag. Section 3 of the Austrian enabling act required the government to lay its decrees before the Reichsrat and to withdraw them on its demand. Both checks were textually sound, neither was ever amended or repealed, and both failed because the body that operates them can be stopped from sitting. Austria did not even need a legal instrument for this: the National Council was prevented from reconvening in March 1933 by police action, and there is no gazette text for it at all.

**The scope is obeyed and evaded.** The limit is respected on the dimension it names while the effect is achieved on a dimension it does not. Article 48(2) permitted suspension of seven enumerated rights articles, and the Reichstag Fire Decree suspended exactly those seven, scrupulously. Article 48(2) also said the suspension was to be *vorübergehend*, temporary. The decree suspended them *bis auf weiteres*, until further notice, and it never ended. The enumeration was honoured to the article; the adverb was dropped. That the substitution was deliberate rather than careless is demonstrable inside the decree itself, because Section 2 uses *vorübergehend* correctly two paragraphs later. The drafter had the constitution's word to hand and did not use it where the constitution required it.

**The duration is limited but renewable, or self-sealing.** The Enabling Act expired on April 1, 1937 and was renewed, and a sunset restrains only an actor who will not hold the power at sunset. Its second termination condition is the sharper one: the Act was also to lapse if the government were replaced by another. That makes the power's continuation conditional on the holder not being displaced, which hands the holder a written incentive to prevent its own displacement using the power it is trying to keep. A termination condition that can only be triggered by an event the power exists to prevent is not a limit on the holder. It is a statement of what the holder must avoid.

The consequence for method is a change of question. Asking whether a document contains checks is close to useless, because all three of these documents did. The question that separates them is who must be functioning for a given check to fire, and whether that person or body can be stopped by someone the check is aimed at.

## What this partition does not cover

Nothing here addresses whether a step is politically likely, and nothing here addresses conduct that breaks a rule. A takeover carried out by force is out of scope for this project by definition, and violence appears in these cases as the thing that made lawful steps effective rather than as a substitute for them. Where an analysis needs an extra-legal step to complete its chain, that is not a gap in this taxonomy. It is a disqualified candidate.
