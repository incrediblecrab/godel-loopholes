# What counts as a finding

This file exists because the cheapest thing an automated process can do is produce many confident claims quickly. Constitutional text is long, suggestive, and full of clauses that can be made to sound sinister in isolation. Without a standard fixed in advance, this project would fill up with plausible readings that no one can check and no one can refute, and the volume would be mistaken for progress.

The standard is fixed here, before any candidates exist, so that it cannot be adjusted afterward to admit one we like.

## What every candidate must state

**The text and the recension.** Which document, which vantage, and which printing. This is not pedantry in this project. Six authoritative witnesses to Amendments I through X disagree on punctuation, one federal document corrupts the First Amendment, and the Statutes at Large prints the Seventh two different ways inside one volume. An argument that turns on a comma must say whose comma.

**The path, as numbered steps.** Each step is a legal operation with a citation to the clause that authorizes it. Not "Congress could then decline to seat members" but the clause that makes seating a matter for the chamber alone. A step without a citation is a wish.

**The legal status of each step**, marked as settled, contested, or novel. Contested steps carry the strongest published argument on the other side, named and cited from `academia/` where one exists.

**The minimum coalition.** How many actors, holding which offices, are required. This single number does more to place a candidate than any amount of prose around it.

**The falsifier.** What would have to be true for this to be wrong: a case, a clause, a historical instance where the path was available and did not work.

## Disqualifiers

**Any step breaks a rule.** Then it is not this project's claim. Lawlessness needs no loophole and a document cannot be examined for it.

**You cannot name the edit that closes it.** If no change to the text would shut the path, the defect is not in the text. You have described what people can do to each other, and every constitution permits that. This is the single most useful filter here and most candidates should die on it.

**It requires only bad faith and not the text.** Related but distinct. A path that any sufficiently determined actor could walk through any document is a fact about power, not about this document.

**The minimum coalition is enormous.** A path needing two chambers and thirty-eight state legislatures is not a loophole. It is Article V working as designed. Gödel's claim, as Morgenstern records it, was that somebody could do this. The smaller the coalition, the more the candidate is worth.

**It is unfalsifiable.** If no observation would count against it, it is not yet an argument.

**It is already in the literature and uncited.** Not fatal, but it must be found and named. The likeliest way this project embarrasses itself is announcing something a law review settled decades ago.

## A chain is as strong as its weakest step

Not as strong as its average, and not stronger for being longer. Five steps each individually arguable do not combine into one strong argument. They combine into a compound of five contested claims, which is weaker than any one of them alone.

A candidate that needs four contested steps should be recorded as what it is: an interesting reading with four places it fails. That is still worth writing down. It is not worth presenting as a discovery.

## Whose judgment decides that it is destruction

The document's own, wherever it speaks. Prefer a constitution's stated purposes, its entrenchments, and the things it declares unamendable over our own sense of what is bad. Where a document entrenches something, it has told us directly what it regards as its own destruction, and that is far better evidence than our intuitions.

This matters because most bad outcomes a constitution permits are not loopholes. They are ordinary politics that the ratifiers accepted the risk of. The claim is not that a path leads somewhere bad. It is that a path leads somewhere the document itself rules out, by means the document itself supplies.

## Null results are recorded in the same detail

A path that was explored and closed is written up with its steps and the reason it closed. This is not consolation. The closed paths are how anyone reading later can tell that the open ones were not simply the first thing we thought of.
